# Support assistant with long-term memory and staged rollout

We need a diagram of how our customer support assistant works and how new versions of it reach production. A customer converses with the assistant through our web and mobile chat surfaces. On every turn, the assistant gathers what it needs before answering: the customer's long-term profile — preferences, past issues, prior resolutions accumulated across earlier conversations — the current conversation so far, and the most relevant help-center articles found by searching the support corpus. A model call produces the reply from that assembled context. When the reply requires an action, such as issuing a refund or changing a subscription, the assistant invokes integrations with the billing and account systems; the credentials for those integrations live in a managed secrets vault and are fetched per invocation, never stored with the assistant itself. A safety layer screens both the customer's input and the assistant's outgoing reply. A screened-out reply is replaced with a safe fallback, and repeated safety triggers or an explicit customer request hand the conversation to a human support agent together with the transcript, the profile context, and what has already been attempted.

After a conversation ends, an extraction pass reviews the transcript and writes durable facts into the customer's profile — a distillation, not a raw log. A new fact that contradicts an older one supersedes it, with the source conversation recorded so any fact can be traced to where it was learned. Raw conversations older than the retention window leave the live store for cold storage, where they remain recoverable for compliance review but are never pulled into a live turn.

The team improves the assistant by proposing challenger variants — a different prompt, retrieval policy, or model choice — that run alongside the production champion. Variant definitions are versioned configuration, so a rollout changes configuration rather than redeploying the service. A small sampled share of live conversations is served by a challenger instead of the champion. A panel of automated judges scores completed champion and challenger conversations on resolution, factual grounding against the profile and the cited articles, and tone, and their verdicts accumulate into an experiment report per challenger. Promotion is gated on people, not just scores: when a challenger clears the bar, a product owner reviews the report and approves or rejects promotion. Approval makes the challenger the new champion, while the displaced champion is retained so a single rollback step can restore it.

Live quality monitoring runs independently of the experiment: it tracks resolution rate, human-handoff rate, and safety triggers per variant, and a challenger that regresses on any of these is pulled from traffic immediately rather than waiting for its experiment to conclude. The diagram should make clear how a customer turn flows through profile and article retrieval, guarded generation, and credentialed actions; how finished conversations become durable profile facts and eventually cold history; and how challenger variants are compared against the champion, promoted only with human approval, and rolled back or pulled when they regress.
